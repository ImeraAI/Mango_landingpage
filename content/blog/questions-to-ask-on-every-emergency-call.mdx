Second trips kill margin. Most of them trace back to something nobody asked on the first call.

## The script

1. **Where is the problem?** Get the full service address, including unit number and gate codes.
2. **What is happening right now?** In the caller's own words, before offering any diagnosis.
3. **When did it start?** A heater that has been out for three days is a different job from one that failed an hour ago, and it changes how you schedule.
4. **What is the make and model?** A photo of the label texted over takes the caller thirty seconds and saves your tech a trip to the supplier.
5. **Has anything been shut off already?** Water, gas, or the breaker.
6. **Who will be on site, and how do we reach them?**
7. **Anything else the tech should know before arriving?** This is where you find out about the dog, the locked side gate, and the elderly parent upstairs.

## Why it works

Seven questions, about ninety seconds. Written down and asked the same way every time, they are worth more than most software.

The catch is consistency: the script only pays off if every call gets it, including the ones that come in at 11pm. That is the same problem as [covering after hours](/blog/after-hours-calls-without-hiring).
