Every trade business has a number it does not track: the calls nobody picked up. Marketing spend gets measured to the dollar, van wraps get argued over, and meanwhile the phone rings at 6:40pm and goes to voicemail.

## The math is simpler than it looks

Take your average job value, multiply by the share of first-time callers who book, and multiply that by the calls you miss in a month.

| Input | Typical shop |
| --- | --- |
| Average job value | $420 |
| First-time callers who book | 45% |
| Missed calls per month | 60 |
| **Lost revenue per month** | **$11,340** |

For a lot of shops that lands somewhere between one and four jobs a week walking straight to a competitor.

## Missed calls are not evenly distributed

The part that stings is *when* they happen. They cluster in exactly the moments when the caller has an emergency and the least patience:

- evenings, after the office closes
- weekends, when the on-call phone is with whoever drew the short straw
- the middle of a job, when your hands are full

> Emergency callers rarely leave a voicemail. They hang up and dial the next result.

That is why a missed call is not a delayed job, it is a lost one — and usually a lost customer for every job after it too.

## Start by counting

Pull your call log for last month and count the unanswered inbound calls. Most owners are surprised, and the number alone usually makes the decision about [what to do next](/blog/after-hours-calls-without-hiring) fairly obvious.
